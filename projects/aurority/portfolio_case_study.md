# Portfolio Case Study

Aurority is a fictional fast-growing digital services company with 62 customer-facing employees. Quality was inconsistent, Sales→Ops handovers created incidents, Slack carried important decisions, and leadership reporting showed outcomes without drivers.

I designed a Quality Operations system around a simple idea: **a trigger starts an investigation; it never equals a conclusion.**

The central layer connects CSAT, NPS, churn signals, support, product usage, deals and revenue with ownership, cross-functional requests, actions and rechecks.

### Northstar Labs
A €72k ARR account approaching renewal shows usage decline, CSAT 2/5 and two open cases. Support investigates first. QA confirms the agents followed documented process; the blocker is Product. Support sends an interdepartmental request, Product responds in the same issue thread, and Support retains customer ownership while Product owns technical resolution.

### Commercial anomaly
A decline in Enterprise win rate routes to Operations for diagnosis. Ops identifies where the funnel changed before involving Sales, Pricing or Product.

### Handover spike
Sales handover failures rise from roughly 6% to 19% and cluster around one field. Because the issue is distributed across the team, Operations treats it as a process/system problem first and solves it with a short cross-functional working session.

Aurority's role is not to make leadership decisions. It makes signals visible, ownership explicit, communication loops closed and outcomes measurable.
